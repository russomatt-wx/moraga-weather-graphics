How spots are rated

SF Bay and harbors: Go under 12 mph (10 kt) with gusts under 18. Caution at 12–17 mph or gusts to 25, or with a Small Craft Advisory. No-go at 17 mph (15 kt) or more. Chop is estimated from wind speed and the open water upwind of each spot (fetch-limited wave growth): about 1½ ft is Caution and 2½ ft or more is No-go. It is a rough guide, not a measurement. Tidal current (NOAA predictions from the nearest current station within about 2 miles): 1½ knots or more is Caution, 3 knots or more is No-go, and wind of 12 mph or more blowing against a current of 1 knot or more is Caution, because that is what stands up steep chop near the Gate. Spots without a nearby current station show estimated chop instead. These follow common paddling guidance: novices under about 10 knots, and over 15 knots is too much for a SUP.

Open coast: Go with waves under 3.5 ft and wind under 12 mph. Caution with waves to 6 ft, wind to 18 mph or any beach or rip current advisory. No-go with waves of 6 ft or more, a High Surf Advisory, or wind above 18 mph. Offshore wind on the coast pushes you away from the beach: treat it with extra care.

Rain: a 50% or higher chance of rain in a 3-hour block makes it at least Caution (cold, low visibility, gusty squalls). Any thunderstorm is No-go: get off the water at the first thunder. After heavy rain, county health departments advise staying out of the water for about 72 hours because of runoff.

Water temperature: dress for the water, not the air. Bay Area water is usually below 60°F, where a wetsuit or drysuit is recommended.

A Gale, Storm or Hazardous Seas warning is No-go everywhere. Ratings are a starting point, not a safety call: check conditions on the water, know the currents at your launch (ebb tides through the Golden Gate run fast), wear a PFD and dress for the water temperature.

Data: Open-Meteo wind and marine forecasts, NOAA CO-OPS tide predictions and water temperature, National Weather Service alerts. Updated hourly.
